# Problem

**Stage 1 — Identify the limits of your current agency.**

## The problem, in your words

Planning a multi-day trip in India is slow, opaque, and stressful. Take Priya: she's in
Chennai, has 5 days and ₹50,000, and wants to take her husband and elderly parents
somewhere green with good food. To plan it herself she has to juggle YouTube videos,
travel blogs, Google Maps, hotel sites, and train/bus schedules for *hours* — and even
then she can't confidently answer the questions that actually matter:

- Is a 5-day Munnar + Thekkady trip *realistic* for two seniors, or is it too much travel?
- What will it *actually* cost, end to end — not a brochure price?
- Is June a good time to go, or will the monsoon wash out the viewpoints?
- Which attractions are worth it, and how do I order them so we're not backtracking?

The usual shortcut is a travel agency — but agencies add a **15–40% markup**, sell
**rigid packages** you can't customize, and never explain *why*. A ₹20,000 trip quietly
becomes ₹30,000, the driver earns less, and the traveler never learns the real prices.

So today the choice is: spend hours researching and still guess, or pay a big markup for
a one-size-fits-all package. Neither lets a normal person plan a trip that is *realistic,
honestly priced, and shaped around them.*

## Why an agent (not just a script or a human)

The hard part isn't looking things up — it's **reasoning across many constraints at
once**: budget vs. days vs. group fitness vs. season vs. crowd vs. travel time vs.
interests, and catching when they conflict ("18 attractions in 2 days is not real").
A static website can only show options; it can't judge feasibility or explain trade-offs.
A script can't handle "actually, my parents can't walk much — adjust." A human travel
consultant *can* do all this — that's exactly the expertise an LLM can replicate
conversationally, without the markup. The intelligence buys us **realism, explanation,
and personalization** — the three things a booking site and a rigid package both lack.
